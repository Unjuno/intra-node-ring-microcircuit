# Autonomous follow-up — mechanism and stopping report (2026-08-03)

## Question

After the primary ten-seed replication failed to establish a ring-specific advantage, the follow-up asked which component, if any, explains the remaining positive results:

1. internal topology;
2. recurrent update depth;
3. multiple subnodes per macro-unit;
4. a learned nonlinear transformation inside each scalar unit.

The stopping rule was: continue only while a new, testable mechanism was supported; otherwise publish the negative result and stop.

## Stage 1 — topology screening

A three-seed screen compared `none`, `ring`, `chain`, `star`, `complete`, and degree-matched `random` topologies at five subnodes and three recurrent steps.

- On Digits, Ring and Chain were close; no topology had a stable, clearly separated advantage.
- On Breast Cancer, all connected topologies produced the same rounded test accuracy in the three screening seeds.
- The screen therefore did not support a ring-specific inductive bias.

These results are exploratory because `n=3`.

## Stage 2 — recurrent-step control

Ring and no-edge units were compared at `T = 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8`.

- Most of the improvement occurred from `T=0` to `T=1`.
- Increasing depth beyond one to three steps produced little additional gain.
- Ring-minus-no-edge differences remained small.

This shifted the hypothesis from “ring topology helps” to “a richer learned unit-level nonlinear transformation may help.”

## Stage 3 — fixed-budget subnode study

The earlier subnode-count screen was invalid for causal interpretation because parameter counts varied with `k`. The experiment was repeated at approximately 30,000 trainable FP32 parameters, with all conditions within about one percent of the target.

On Digits, the no-edge micro-unit improved over MLP for `k=1`, `k=2`, and `k=5`. Because `k=1` has no multi-state topology, the result cannot be attributed to multiple subnodes or ring communication.

A seven-seed confirmatory split using seeds not used in screening gave:

| Contrast | Mean difference | 95% CI | Exact sign-flip p | Wins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `k1_none - MLP`, Digits | +1.94 pp | `[+1.37,+2.52]` | 0.015625 | 7/7 |
| `k2_none - MLP`, Digits | +1.75 pp | `[+1.31,+2.18]` | 0.015625 | 7/7 |
| `k5_none - MLP`, Digits | +1.27 pp | `[+1.02,+1.52]` | 0.015625 | 7/7 |
| `k5_ring - k5_none`, Digits | -0.12 pp | `[-0.26,+0.02]` | 0.25 | 0/7, four ties |

Breast Cancer did not confirm a general benefit at the larger fixed budget. Results varied with `k`, and the five-subnode variants were not superior to MLP.

## Stage 4 — ordinary architecture controls

Two alternatives were tested under the same approximate 30,000-parameter budget on Digits:

- `Composite`: the standard two-layer MLP with the fixed activation `GELU(tanh(z))`;
- `Deep4`: a four-hidden-layer GELU MLP.

Across ten paired seeds:

| Contrast | Mean difference | 95% CI | Exact p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `Composite - MLP` | +1.06 pp | `[+0.63,+1.48]` | 0.003906 |
| `Deep4 - MLP` | +0.47 pp | `[-0.35,+1.29]` | 0.253906 |
| legacy `k1_none - Composite` | +0.61 pp | `[+0.33,+0.89]` | 0.003906 |

A fixed composite activation explains part, but not all, of the gain. Simply making the MLP deeper did not reproduce it.

## Stage 5 — dedicated scalar micro-activation

The legacy `k=1` implementation still contained dormant neighbor and one-element readout parameters. A dedicated implementation removed those unused parameters. Each unit computes a learned scalar transformation:

1. project to a scalar preactivation;
2. apply `tanh`;
3. apply one or three learned self/drive updates;
4. pass the result through the outer GELU.

The model was matched to approximately 30,000 trainable parameters.

### Digits, ten paired seeds

| Model | Accuracy | Latency, us/sample |
|---|---:|---:|
| MLP | 96.67% | 1.11 |
| Composite | 97.72% | 1.88 |
| ScalarMicro `T=1` | 98.28% | 2.77 |
| ScalarMicro `T=3` | 98.31% | 4.72 |

Key paired contrasts:

| Contrast | Mean difference | 95% CI | Exact p | Wins/ties/losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ScalarMicroT1 - MLP` | +1.61 pp | `[+1.19,+2.03]` | 0.001953 | 10/0/0 |
| `ScalarMicroT3 - MLP` | +1.64 pp | `[+1.18,+2.10]` | 0.001953 | 10/0/0 |
| `ScalarMicroT1 - Composite` | +0.56 pp | `[+0.26,+0.85]` | 0.001953 | 10/0/0 |
| `ScalarMicroT1 - ScalarMicroT3` | -0.03 pp | `[-0.14,+0.09]` | 1.0 | 1/7/2 |

Three updates did not improve on one update.

Here, extra recurrent steps were harmful.

## External screen

A five-seed Wine screen was inconclusive. `k1_none - MLP` averaged +1.11 pp, but the confidence interval crossed zero. The result must not be presented as a Wine improvement.

## Final interpretation

### Supported

- The tested ring topology does not have a demonstrated advantage.
- Multiple internal subnodes are not necessary for the positive Digits result.
- A dedicated learned scalar micro-activation improved Digits and the synthetic cyclic-interaction task under closely matched parameter budgets.
- A single learned update was sufficient; repeated updates added cost and did not improve Digits, while harming the synthetic task.
- The learned scalar micro-activation is slower than the baseline MLP.

### Not supported

- General superiority across datasets.
- A ring-specific effect.
- A multi-state or graph-message-passing explanation for the positive result.
- Compute or latency efficiency.
- Claims about anomaly localization, causal interpretation, robustness, or biological fidelity.

## Research pivot

The evidence supports a pivot from “ring microcircuit neurons” to the narrower hypothesis:

> A parameter-matched MLP can benefit on some tasks when its fixed pointwise activation is replaced by a small learned scalar state-update function.

This belongs closer to adaptive or learnable activation functions than to graph topology research. A prior-art search is required before making novelty claims.

## Stopping decision

The autonomous search stops here because the original ring hypothesis has been falsified, and the remaining positive mechanism has been isolated sufficiently for an exploratory submission:

- positive replicated result on two tasks;
- negative or uncertain results disclosed;
- ordinary activation and depth controls included;
- unused-parameter artifact removed;
- recurrence-depth effect separated;
- parameter and latency trade-offs reported.

Further work is optional rather than necessary for an honest repository release. The next scientifically justified project would be a dedicated comparison against modern adaptive activation-function baselines, not additional ring-topology tuning.
